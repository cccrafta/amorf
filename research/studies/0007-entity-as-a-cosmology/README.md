# STU-0007: An entity as a cosmology: a form and its satellites as a model of a piece of knowledge

Starts from the owner's verdict on [EXP-0018](../../experiments/0018-satellite-forms-around-the-entity-form/README.md), 2026-11-06: the floating form and its satellites "show a different cosmology of what would else be just a collection of images and pictures and media… its movement makes it alive and unique. It is a new way to approach articles and pieces of knowledge." The proposal tested is [DDR-0005](../../../docs/decisions/ddr/0005-entity-as-thumbnail-form-with-satellite-forms.md).

## Question

What separates a cosmology (bodies held in relation by a law, in motion) from a collection (items in a list or grid), which tradition of modelling knowledge as a cosmos the forms lab now sits in, and what that asks of the [entity](../../theory/ontology/entity.md) and the [field](../../theory/ontology/field.md).

## Sources

The EXP-0018 verdict is primary. The others are cited from standing knowledge of well-known works and have not been re-read for this study; each note says what is taken from it. Read in full before any deduction becomes a decision: Yates, Benjamin's prologue, Manovich, Bush, Heider and Simmel.

## Notes

What the sources say. Readings for amorf are in Deductions.

**1. The cosmos as a model you can hold.**

- **The orrery.** The orrery (Rowley's, made for the Earl of Orrery around 1712, after Graham) is a model of the solar system in which the bodies' relative sizes, distances and speeds are the whole content. Wright of Derby's painting of a lecture on one shows it as an instrument of understanding, gathered around, not a picture looked at.
- **Kepler.** Kepler's *Mysterium Cosmographicum* explained the planets' spacing by nesting the five regular solids, the cube among them, between their spheres: a cosmos whose structure is geometry. His third law (1619), period squared as distance cubed, is the law the lab's orbits use (`ORBIT_K`, `orbit.ts`).

**2. The cosmos as a model of memory.**

- **Camillo's theatre.** Camillo's memory theatre, as Yates describes it, arranged all knowledge in a built amphitheatre ordered by the seven planets, so that a place stood for an idea and the order of places was the order of knowledge.

**3. Constellation against collection.**

- **Benjamin.** Benjamin: "Ideas are to objects as constellations are to stars." The idea is not one of the objects nor their sum, but the configuration in which they are seen together.
- **Warburg.** Warburg's Mnemosyne atlas pinned photographs on black panels and rearranged them; the meaning was in the arrangement and its changes, and the atlas was never finished.
- **Manovich.** Manovich names the database (a collection of items, each as significant as any other, with no order) the symbolic form of new media, against narrative (a cause-and-effect trajectory through items). A grid of thumbnails is the database form.

**4. Knowledge as linked bodies.**

- **Otlet and Le Corbusier.** Otlet's documentation broke books into facts on cards linked across a universal classification, and planned the Mundaneum as their building (Le Corbusier's 1929 project, a spiral museum).
- **Bush.** Bush's memex kept "trails", associations between documents a reader made and could pass on.
- **Nelson.** Nelson's hypertext and transclusion hold that knowledge is "deeply intertwingled": a document is its links as much as its text.

**5. Motion read as life.**

- **Heider and Simmel; Michotte.** Heider and Simmel's film of two triangles and a circle moving around a box was described by nearly every viewer as a story of characters with intentions. Michotte showed that timing alone (when one shape moves after another meets it) is seen as cause.
- **Thomas and Johnston.** Thomas and Johnston's principles of animation (slow in and slow out, arcs, follow-through, secondary action) are the means by which drawn things are made to read as alive; mechanical timing reads as dead.
- **Calder.** Calder's mobiles are structures whose balance is the form and whose motion is set by air, never repeated; *A Universe* (1934) is a motor-driven pair of spheres on wire paths, a cosmos as sculpture.

## Deductions

Numbered as in the frontmatter. Each traces to a note.

1. **System, not set** (notes 1, 3). The lab's entity is now an orrery of one piece of knowledge: the cover as the central body, the other media as bodies held around it. A grid of the same pictures is Manovich's database, every item level; the satellites show that these pictures belong to this cover and to each other, before anything is opened, as Benjamin's constellation shows the idea and not the stars.
2. **The orbit is the only ornament** (note 1, manifesto). Nothing in the cluster is decorative. How many satellites is how many pictures; how far and how fast follows Kepler's law, which Kepler himself used to read structure in the heavens. The look is the consequence of the data and the law, which is the thesis applied to media rather than to a building.
3. **Motion is identity** (note 5). Every entity runs the same law with its own seeded numbers (`satellites.ts`), so no two move alike. People read moving shapes as agents before they read them as diagrams (Heider and Simmel), so the motion is the first thing that makes an entity "alive and unique", in the owner's words. An entity becomes recognisable by its behaviour, which a static thumbnail cannot do.
4. **Lawful motion or none** (note 5, EXP-0018). In EXP-0018 the owner rejected an overshooting spring ("a micro adjustment") and a fixed camera route ("a locked sequence"), and accepted a curved flight on a front-loaded ease. These are the animators' arcs and slow in and slow out. A correction or a stop reads as a machine at work and breaks the reading of life, so every motion in the field is continuous from where a body is, never restarted from a set pose.
5. **Opening shows the becoming** (note 3, manifesto). The cubes fly from their orbits to the board and back, flattening on the way. The reader watches the cosmology turn into the reading surface, so the board is not a new page but the same bodies laid out. It is Warburg's rearrangement made continuous, and the process stays visible next to the artifact.
6. **Distance should carry rank** (notes 2, 4). Camillo's theatre and Otlet's classification put knowledge in an order of places. The lab places satellites by a hash of the entity id, which gives a stable but meaningless order (the field already holds that placement by id hash does not carry meaning, STU-0003). The author's order of the media should decide which body is nearest, largest or first to fly.
7. **An article is a system too** (note 4). Nothing in the model is specific to pictures. An article's text can be the body; its figures, sections and sources the satellites; its links to other entities the trails between systems across the field, as Bush and Nelson describe. This is how the field could hold writing and research, not only images, with one form family (STU-0006 deduction 9).
8. **Not a spectacle** (note 5, STU-0003). The same motion that makes an entity alive can make the field a screensaver. Forms already calm and face the reader up close (`nearCalm`, `SETTLE_NEAR`); at field distance motion must stay slow, as STU-0003 asks for designed, slow pace, and must never hide what it carries. Whether the clusters read at field distance is EXP-0018 prediction 1, still untested.
