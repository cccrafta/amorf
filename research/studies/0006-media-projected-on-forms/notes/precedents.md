# Forms with projected media: precedents and design reasoning (for STU-0006)

Date 2026-11-06. Web search only (no full-page reads); claims from search summaries. "[unverified]" = not backed by a fetched source or my own inference.

## 1. Art and architecture precedents (kept: those that say something about how media on a form reads)

### Orthographic "glass box" and the cube net (strongest link)
- Multiview projection: the object sits in a six-sided glass box, each face receives one view (front, back, top, bottom, left, right), the box is unfolded into a cross. Top/front/bottom align vertically, rear/left/front/right horizontally. The net IS the exploded view. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiview_orthographic_projection ; https://motenv.wordpress.com/2024/11/14/glass-box-method-of-orthographic-projection/ ; Monge history https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptive_geometry
- Wikipedia notes a "developed elevation": adjacent non-parallel sides shown together as if unfolded. Same page.
- Robin Evans, "Translations from Drawing to Building": architecture works through projection, orthographic drawing as translation toward construction, not picture. https://arch2281sp14.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/robin-evans-1997-translations-from-drawing-to-building.pdf
- Teaching: CUNY City Tech exercise asks for photos of all 6 elevations plus the unfolded-elevations drawing. https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/etioarch1112fa20/project-2/ (coursework only; I found NO exhibition showing a building's drawings on the faces of a physical cube).

### Hejduk (drawings as object; faces as plan/elevation)
- Wall House 1: axonometrics showing N, S, E, W as a series of elevations; 90-degree axonometric reads as "folding or hinging more than projection", plan tipped up into elevation, roof rendered as elevation. https://drawingmatter.org/drawing-parallels-john-hejduk-wall-house-1/ ; https://drawingmatter.org/john-hejduks-axonometric-degree-zero/
- CCA quote: "Plans become elevations and sections; the square frame of the plan becomes a wall." https://www.cca.qc.ca/en/events/58759/lists-and-lists-and
- Nine-square grid / Texas Houses: one 3D "canvas" varied across houses (form constant, content varies). https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/9590/8172/47365 ; Eisenman essay https://burning.farm/essays/in-my-father-s-house-are-many-mansions
- Masques: structures as individual "characters" each with its own form, specified by relationships. https://socks-studio.com/2015/11/01/a-growing-incremental-place-incremental-time-victims-a-project-by-john-hejduk-1984/ (relevant to form-per-type; see section 5)

### Axonometric / exploded
- Choisy worm's-eye: plan, section, interior, elevation read in one drawing; Stan Allen contrasts 90-degree axonometric (building as object) with worm's-eye (interior space). https://drawingmatter.org/review-situated-objects/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_Choisy. Allen notes axonometry lends itself to sectioned/exploded views (no source for Choisy exploding specifically).
- Eisenman House VI: axonometric series as step-by-step account of a single form's internal logic. https://www.artforum.com/events/perfect-acts-of-architecture-2-206143/ ; Getty "Axonometric of cube" https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/component/10PSZ8
- Tschumi: takes a cube and explodes/dissects it (Villette); exploded axonometrics as tool; Transcripts combine plan/section/photo/notation to record events not shown by usual drawings. https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_423_300063112.pdf ; https://www.moma.org/collection/works/19

### Sol LeWitt, Incomplete Open Cubes (1974)
- 122 frame cubes, each also drawn as a diagram on a matrix; rules (one edge per axis, joined, rotationally unique); all as sculpture + drawing + photos + book. Says: a cube as a unit of a recorded, rule-bound series where process (the rules) is the work. Fits amorf's "process over artifact". Also a caution: open frame, not a surface to project on. https://www.artgallery.nsw.gov.au/collection/works/349.2011/ ; https://socks-studio.com/2011/04/23/variations-of-incomplete-cubes-by-sol-lewitt/ ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00004-015-0254-8

### Eames multi-screen
- Glimpses of the USA (1959): seven screens; number chosen so viewers "wouldn't be exactly sure how many they have seen"; four you always count, eight you lose count. Purpose: information overload as persuasion, plus proof that things exist. https://www.bauhaus-imaginista.org/articles/5926/the-design-of-information-overload/ ; https://enso.readymag.com/eames/glimpses-of-the-usa/
- Think (1964): 22 screens of varied shape (circle, square, triangle, rectangle) mixing film and slides. https://eamesfoundation.org/work/think/ (sources disagree whether simultaneous). Lesson: more than ~6 simultaneous images stops being reading and becomes impression; screen shape variety was used.

### Richter, Atlas
- Photographs, clippings, sketches pasted on standardised sheets in a rigid grid from 1969; ~800 panels / 5000+ images; the grid is the neutral matrix, the order is partly unsystematic ("anomic archive", Buchloh; contested by RIHA). Lesson: the flat board = grid of equal cells; heterogeneous media stays readable because the container is uniform. https://www.qagoma.qld.gov.au/stories/the-order-of-memory-gerhard-richters-atlas/ ; https://www.diaart.org/about/press/gerhard-richter-atlas-at-dia-center-for-the-arts/type/html ; https://d-nb.info/1176034227/34

### Projection on volumes
- Tony Oursler, Eyes: eyes/faces projected on suspended fiberglass spheres, gesso balls, doll forms; the volume is a body, content is a face. Works because ONE image on ONE sphere, and the sphere is semantically a head/eye. Not an archive. https://eyecontactmagazine.com/2012/07/projected-oursler-sculptures ; legibility note (robot face sphere): density ~2000 px at top ring vs ~200 px at equator, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.00662
- 555 KUBIK (Urbanscreen, 2009, Ungers' Galerie der Gegenwart, a cube-shaped building): content derives from the building's own geometry ("how it would be if a house was dreaming"); mapped from one projection point as seen by the render camera. Lesson: projected content should answer the form's structure (edges, grid), not ignore it; also viewpoint-dependent. https://www.urbanscreen.com/555-kubik/ ; https://klofmag.com/2011/09/art-555-kubik-facade-projection/
- Planetarium/dome: avoid zenith ("hardest place to look at"); keep primary content within roughly +-45-60 deg of equator, no text at poles; fisheye looks undistorted only from the intended viewpoint. https://www.utsubo.com/blog/led-sphere-dome-screen-content-guide ; https://paulbourke.net/dome/faq/ ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/239921171_Live_Action_Film_Footage_for_an_Astronomy_Fulldome_Show
- Eliasson, Model room: ~450 models (cardboard, wire, Lego), displayed on a nonorthogonal system as a "reference library"; geometric forms as research archive. Says: forms as the record of process. https://olafureliasson.net/artwork/model-room-2003/

### Dropped
Albers/Bauhaus: searches only found Bauhaus paper-folding exercises (surface to 3D object, "respect the material"); marginal, no cube-net evidence. https://www.bauhaus-imaginista.org/articles/5176/three-preliminary-courses-itten-moholy-nagy-albers/ . Eliasson light works: nothing on media on form.

### Legibility numbers
- Text/image rotation 0-60 deg off-normal has little effect on reading; beyond 60 deg readability drops (Buttner et al. 2020, via secondary citations; full text not read). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/341319007_The_influence_of_text_rotation_font_and_distance_on_legibility_in_VR ; Grossman et al. CHI 2007 https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~ravin/papers/chi2007_volumetrictextorientation.pdf ; Wei et al. 2020: warp text one direction, small curvature (via search summary).
- Consequence for a cube (my geometry, not a source): at a 3/4 view, front face ~35 deg off normal, side ~55 deg; foreshortening cos(55)=0.57 width. Third face reads poorly; at exactly front view the sides vanish. A cube read from the field can legibly show 1 face, at most 2. Top face is seen at the walker's eye height only when the camera is above the form.
- Sphere: equirectangular texel density ~ 1/cos(lat), infinite at poles; cubemap density varies ~5.2x centre to corner; every flattening distorts. Sources: https://www.playtex.ai/guides/equirectangular-vs-cubemap (figures I stated from general maths [unverified]). From outside, only one hemisphere is visible, and the visible rim is foreshortened strongly: the readable cap is roughly the central 60 deg. If a panorama is mapped on the sphere seen from outside it needs the U flipped or it reads mirrored (my reasoning, based on https://gist.github.com/nKolev91/675cf8143e96de50f5ed).

## 3. Mapping rules (reasoned)

- Number of faces vs media: Eames reached seven so viewers lose count; amorf wants a count that can be taken in: 6 faces is already at the limit of simultaneously seen content, and only 1-2 faces are visible at once from any viewpoint.
- Front face = cover image (media[0] or an explicitly marked cover) because it is the only face guaranteed in view in the field; the glass-box convention has the front view as the datum and the others derived around it (cross layout).
- Net convention: the standard cross (top above front, bottom below, back/left/right horizontal) gives a deterministic, drawing-literate relationship between face and position, and flat view = net.
- Non-square media on square faces: contain (letterbox) for drawings/plans/text (never crop information-bearing media: Evans, orthographic drawing is notation); cover-crop only for photos where the crop is chosen by the author. [design reasoning; no direct precedent]. Richter Atlas: uniform cell, heterogeneous contents inside, with the image sized in the cell rather than the cell shaped by the image.
- Media count > 6: first 6 on faces, rest only in the flat/exploded view (or per-face carousel/slideshow on dwell). Media count < 6: leave unfilled faces as the neutral material (honest blank, the thesis); do not repeat images to fill. Count 1: cube with one face + neutral body, or a plane. [reasoned]
- Video on faces: play only the front-facing face when near and in view; poster frame otherwise (carousel evidence: hidden faces are not seen anyway; also performance). Sound: not tied to a face; a nearby entity emits spatialised audio at the form centre, never required (STU-0005 rule).
- Top/bottom faces: top is seen only from above (plan camera); reserve for plan/drawings/map-like media. Bottom is almost never seen: use for metadata/colophon or leave blank. Hejduk: plan tipped into elevation; roof rendered as elevation.

## 4. Cube vs sphere semantically

- Cube = discrete, ordered, directional, orthographic: fits plan/elevation/section; the glass-box and Hejduk both treat the box as the container of views. Architectural media naturally fit: elevations on sides (N/S/E/W), roof plan on top, ground floor plan on bottom, section = needs a cut so does not fit a face; the section is the inside view (an "inside" experience, or a cut-away form) [reasoned]. I found NO precedent of a physical or web "building as a cube of its drawings"; the closest are Hejduk's N/S/E/W axonometric series and the glass-box convention.
- Sphere = continuous: panorama, 360 photo, planetarium; Oursler's single image/eye. Fits equirectangular media, a globe, or sound/field recordings (spatial). Poor for ordered discrete lists and for text/drawings (distortion, readable cap ~central 60 deg, dome guidance: no content at poles).
- Hence: cube = the default form for ordered media sets and drawing sets; sphere = for entities whose primary medium is a 360/panorama or a continuous capture, or as a deliberate variant.

## 5. Form encodes type, or one form for all

- Bertin/Mackinlay: shape is an identity (nominal) channel, good for categories, ranks after position, hue, texture for nominal data; so form-per-type is valid but cannot encode ordered or quantitative things; also only a handful of shapes (usually quoted 5-7) stay discriminable [unverified]. https://colorado.pressbooks.pub/makingmaps/chapter/chapter-4-visual-variables/ ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.01271
- Hejduk Masques: a vocabulary of distinct forms as characters (tower, theatre, maze) shows form-per-role works when the set of roles is small and authored. Texas Houses show the opposite: one constant form, variation of content, is a research method (comparison). 
- Recommendation (reasoning): one form family (cube) for all entities for now, with variation by what is on it and optionally proportion (a box, not strictly a cube, e.g. 1:1:1 vs flat slab) rather than by a new class of solid; introduce sphere only for a specific media kind (panorama). Do not couple form to entity type until types are stable; keep form as data on the entity (default derived from media kinds).
- The type-by-form idea matches amorf's "structure is the ornament": the form's structure (faces count, orthographic orientation) carries meaning, not decoration.
