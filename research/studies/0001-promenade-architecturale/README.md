# STU-0001: Promenade architecturale

## Question

What is the *promenade architecturale*, and what of it can a digital field and a recorded journey carry? It serves the [field](../../theory/ontology/field.md) (moving through depth, thresholds, sightlines) and the [journey](../../theory/ontology/journey.md) (sequence recorded as a path). Starting point: the ontology conversation, where the term framed the "temporal experience of discovery" ([transcript](../../theory/ontology/transcript.md)).

## Notes

What the sources say. My own reading is kept apart, in italics, under each group.

### The term

- Le Corbusier coined *promenade architecturale* for the implied itinerary through a building. He described it as an "architectural spectacle" unfolding along a route, with changing perspectives and varied light and shadow ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promenade_architecturale)).
- Samuel finds the term first in his description of Villa Savoye. She reads it two ways: as physical movement through the building, and as architecture experienced as an initiation (Samuel 2010, via excerpt).

*Reading: "initiation" implies an order. Some things are seen before others, and the later ones mean more because of the earlier ones. That is close to the ontology's "what you discover depends on where you came from".*

### The houses

- **Maison La Roche (1923-25):** his first promenade inside a house. The route splits into two paths, and each ends in a cul-de-sac (thal.art).
- **Villa Savoye (1928-31):** a ramp rises through the centre. He preferred ramps to stairs because they give continuous movement and a continuously changing view. Several routes reach the roof terrace, which closes the promenade into a circuit (Wikipedia; secondary guides).

*Reading: two topologies. La Roche branches and ends; Savoye loops and climbs. A ramp is a stair with no cuts, the same contrast as a smooth camera move against a jump cut.*

### Choisy and the Acropolis

- Choisy drew the Acropolis as a series of perspectives, each keyed to a position on a plan, so the walk is explained frame by frame (Eisenstein, quoting Choisy).
- He stressed that the views are oblique and "picturesque", not frontal, and that the layout lets one building take the walker's attention after another, in turn.

*Reading: what is composed is the arrangement relative to a moving viewer, not any single building. In amorf's terms that is a property of coordinates and space, not of the entity.*

### Eisenstein: montage made by walking

- Eisenstein calls the Acropolis the most subtly composed montage sequence he can imagine, one "our legs create by walking among the buildings".
- In film the spectator sits still and montage moves the path past them. In architecture the spectator moves and produces the sequence themselves.
- Bois's introduction draws a line from Piranesi through Choisy and Eisenstein to Le Corbusier (via search summaries of Bois).

*Reading: the field sits between Eisenstein's two cases. The visitor moves (architecture), but the camera is software (film). A recorded journey turns the visitor's walk back into a film that can be replayed.*

### The digital line

- *La Promenade Architecturale ... and the Digital World* argues that understanding an environment as a sequence of images met while walking predates the digital. It runs through architecture, film and comics, and now video games. It also proposes 3D walk-throughs as a way to study how spatial features shape experience (abstract).
- Totten (2014) formalises categories of space for level design, including arrivals (summary in a [paper that applies it](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387777019_Architecture_and_Videogame_The_Spatial_Connectivity_and_Digital_Twinning)).
- A [Game Developer essay](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/towards-a-digital-architecture) applies the Savoye habit of climbing and looking down to level design: a high point that shows the whole island.
- *The architectural promenade as narrative device* covers Le Corbusier's own use of film to narrate architecture, with Villa Meyer (1925) as a case (abstract).

*Reading: level design already holds a working vocabulary for the promenade (lures, arrivals, vantage points, gating). It is the nearest practice to the field and should be read before inventing new terms.*

## Deductions

None held yet. Candidates to weigh, each traceable to a note above:

- What is composed is the arrangement relative to the moving visitor, not the entity alone (Choisy).
- The approach to an entity can be oblique and reveal it in stages, rather than present it frontally (Choisy).
- Branching with dead ends (La Roche) and a climbing circuit (Savoye) are two distinct topologies for a space.
- A journey is a montage the visitor made. Recording camera views, not only visited entities, would let it be replayed as one (Eisenstein).
- Level design is the nearest working vocabulary (Totten).
