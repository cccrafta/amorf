**Annotated list**

1. **Cullen, *Townscape* (1961), serial vision, "here and there".** Scenery is revealed "in a series of jerks or revelations", and a straight view is digested and goes monotonous. Move: author the journey as 2-3 held views with a decision between them. Every "there" needs a known "here" (an enclosure around the viewer). https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781136020902_A23833734/preview-9781136020902_A23833734.pdf

2. **Lynch, *The Image of the City* (1960).** Legibility comes from paths, edges, districts, nodes and landmarks. Move: give each curation one landmark visible from afar, one node where the path stops, and edges that bound it. Together they make a place with a shape. https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9780203094235-16/image-environment-city-image-elements-kevin-lynch

3. **Gibson, *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception* (1979), ground theory.** Distance is read from the ground's texture gradient and from occlusion and disocclusion over time. Move: add a textured, material ground plane with a real gradient, and cards that stand on it and occlude each other. The void is what remains when there is no ground. https://classes.matthewjbrown.net/teaching-files/ccc/goldstein-gibson.pdf

4. **Maki, "Japanese city spaces and the concept of oku" (*Japan Architect* 265, 1979).** Oku is the innermost, least accessible core, reached by passing through layers like an onion. Move: put the curation's centre behind two or three layers. Depth is a count of passages, not a camera distance. https://aliceblogs.ch/the-concept-of-japanese-space-and-the-nature-of-the-teahouse-oku/ (essay itself not opened)

5. **Ji Cheng, *Yuan Ye* (c.1631), borrowed scenery (jiejing) and hide-and-reveal.** Views are linked in sequence, alternately concealed and leaked, and the visitor never sees the whole at the start. Frames (moon gates, lattice windows) make depth. Move: show a neighbouring space through an aperture before entering it. https://www.fus.edu/intervalla-files/vol5/2_jones.pdf (the book's date varies by source; the 4-types and "never the whole" points come from secondary sources)

6. **Scarpa, Castelvecchio (1957-64), read by Stavroulaki and Peponis, "The spatial construction of seeing at Castelvecchio" (2003).** The entrance was moved so the rooms form one sequence. The threshold gives an overview, and no statue is seen frontally. A detailed paving edge marks the crossing. Move: stop facing everything at the viewer, and angle objects to the route. Make the sill a visible material joint, which is the thesis applied. https://www.spacesyntax.net/symposia-archive/SSS4/fullpapers/66Stavroulaki-Peponis.pdf

7. **Alexander, *A Pattern Language* (1977), Entrance Transition (112) and Intimacy Gradient (127).** A transition space is "marked with a change of light, sound, direction, surface, level... above all a change of view". Move: build a vestibule with these changes in sequence, and order the space from public to private. https://www.patternlanguageindex.com/patterns/entrance-transition (112 is full text; 127 is paraphrase only)

8. **Tuan, *Space and Place* (1977).** Undifferentiated space becomes place as we get to know it and endow it with value, and "place is pause". Move: make pauses (stops, a seat, a dwell state) part of the mechanic, and let the visitor's own marks accumulate. https://readingandwalking.ca/2019/02/02/20-yi-fu-tuan-space-and-place-the-perspective-of-experience/ (page numbers unverified)

9. **Tschumi, *The Manhattan Transcripts* (1976-81), with Halprin's Motation (1965) and Thiel's sequence-experience notation (1961).** Space, movement and event are notated separately and then superimposed. Move: log journeys as scores, and replay or compose a space from a journey's events, not only its object list. https://www.moma.org/collection/works/19 (Thiel: *Town Planning Review* 32(1), found only as a citation; his book *Visual Awareness and Design* is not verified)

10. **Benedikt, isovists (1979); Hillier and Hanson, *The Social Logic of Space* (1984).** An isovist is the set of points visible from a position, and depth is the number of spaces passed through. Move: use isovist area and depth as design variables per stop. Alternate wide and narrow and vary the depth, instead of everything being visible from everywhere. https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/To-Take-Hold-of-Space:-Isovists-and-Isovist-Fields-Benedikt/36184babf449e1524af4d8eaf71e56dcf05bc659

11. **Pallasmaa, *The Eyes of the Skin* (1996).** "Deep shadows and darkness... make depth and distance ambiguous and invite peripheral vision and tactile fantasy." Move: replace the uniform black with graded light and shadow, with material surfaces that catch it, so the black has structure. https://peterleithart.substack.com/p/eyes-of-the-skin (secondary quotes; check the edition)

12. **Holl, *Questions of Perception* (1994).** Architecture is experienced in partial views that join foreground, middle ground and distance. Move: always keep three depth bands in frame, such as a near jamb, a mid object and a far glimpse. https://www.domusweb.it/en/speciali/guest-editor/steven-holl-toshiko-mori/2023/02/03/steven-holl-questions-of-perception.html (the "parallax" definition comes from his separate book *Parallax*, 2000)

13. **Zumthor, *Atmospheres* (2003): the sound and temperature of a space, "composure and seduction", "tension between interior and exterior".** The experienced quality comes from material, sound, temperature and light. Move: change the ambient sound and tone at the threshold, and let the material set the mood. https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/kent/article/1635364 (secondary; no direct Zumthor text opened)

Dropped: Bachelard. Search gave nothing usable on thresholds.

**Cross-cutting lessons**
1. **A place has a ground, enclosure and a landmark.** A void has none of these. Gibson (ground), Cullen (here/there) and Lynch (landmark and edges) agree that an enclosed volume on a textured plane beats floating objects. Cards facing the viewer remove the ground, the edges and the occlusion that read as depth.
2. **Depth is experienced through partial occlusion.** Every source here (Holl, Maki, Ji Cheng, Scarpa) conceals the whole and shows a fragment through an aperture. Don't show the full curation at once.
3. **A threshold is a change in several channels over a distance.** Alexander lists light, sound, direction, surface, level and view. Scarpa makes the crossing a material joint. A fade changes one channel (opacity) in zero distance. Use a vestibule: compress, darken, change the ground and the sound, then open out.
4. **Orientation should come from the route.** Scarpa's objects are never frontal and Cullen's views are strung along a path. Objects set at oblique angles to the path invite a body, not a screen.
5. **The visitor's pauses and marks turn the space into a place.** Tuan and Tschumi/Halprin point to dwell moments and notated journeys. That fits "process over artifact".
