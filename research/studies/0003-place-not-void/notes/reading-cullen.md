**Cullen's claims (primary, Introduction)**
- Serial vision, p. 9: "although the pedestrian walks through the town at a uniform speed, the scenery of towns is often revealed in a series of jerks or revelations."
- The mechanism is contrast held in memory, p. 9: "when two pictures (the street and the courtyard) are in the mind at the same time, a vivid contrast is felt." Also "A long straight road has little impact because the initial view is soon digested and becomes monotonous."
- The reveal is a step change, p. 9: the new view "is revealed instantaneously at the point of turning, and this view remains with us."
- Existing and emerging view, p. 9: "we have split it into two elements: the existing view and the emerging view." Normally this chain is "accidental", and the designer should "take over this linking". The conclusion (p. 12) calls the second the "Revealed view".
- Place, pp. 9-10: the viewer says "I am outside IT, I am entering IT, I am in the middle of IT". The city becomes "a sequence of exposures and enclosures, of constraint and relief."
- Containment then revelation, p. 10: a street, then a veranda over a drop. "By this device of the containment (street) and the revelation (cantilever) the fact of height is dramatized."
- Here and there, p. 10: "no sooner do we postulate a HERE than automatically we must create a THERE."
- Rashtrapati Bhawan, pp. 10-11: the building is partly hidden by a ramp, so "The building is withheld." It is "gradually revealed" as you climb, then a railing screen re-creates here and there. This is truncation, then a screened vista.
- Casebook captions, pp. 17-19:
  - "even progress of travel is illuminated by a series of sudden contrasts".
  - "slightest deviation in alignment... disproportionally powerful effect in the third dimension".
  - Oxford is "the unfolding of a mystery, the sense that as you press on more is revealed".
  - Ipswich: an archway divides the prospect into "the street you are in and the place beyond".
  - Westminster rewards "an eye which is open and not lazy".

**Named devices (secondary, Place section)**
- Known here, known there (p. 35): pinpointing, change of level, vistas, narrows, closure.
- Known here, unknown there (p. 35): anticipation, infinity, the maw.
- Anticipation (p. 49): "the here is known but the beyond is unknown, is infinite, mysterious, or is hidden inside a black maw."
- Maw (p. 52): "Black, motionless and silent". It is the unknown created by utter blackness.
- Closure: cuts the linear route into digestible stretches while keeping progression. "Travel" versus "arrival" is student-note wording, unverified.
- Enclosure: a static, inward, self-sufficient room. It sits under Possession, with occupied territory, advantage, focal point and viscosity (p. 21).
- Deflection (per caption snippet): an angle change "creates a secondary space by implication... which you cannot see but feel must be there."
- Punctuation and "escapes": glimpses out of an enclosure, so it does not turn claustrophobic. This is RTF's gloss, not Cullen's wording.
- Narrows: no definition found. Presumably a constriction that compresses, then releases.
- Truncation, thereness, incident: named in the outline only.

**Implications for a walkable rendered scene**
- Compose a sequence as pairs of existing view and emerging view. Each reveal is a discrete, near-instant change in what the camera can see, caused by geometry such as a corner, aperture or level change, while camera speed stays constant. Do not use a crossfade or dissolve. Cullen's jerk comes from occlusion changing, not from blending.
- The previous view must remain in memory at the moment of the next. Keep it partly in the periphery, or keep a trace of it behind the viewer, so the contrast registers.
- Enclosure on screen means walls, ceiling and floor define a "room" that the viewer is "in". Aperture, scale and light make the "I am in IT" and "entering IT" states legible. A void does not provide this.
- Translate directly:
  - Narrows: squeeze the aperture before an opening.
  - Truncation: show only the upper part of the next space, as at Rashtrapati Bhawan.
  - Deflection: bend the axis so the next space is implied but not visible.
  - Closure: end each stretch so only a digestible amount is visible.
  - Anticipation and maw: leave the next space dark or hidden, so the unknown is felt.
  - Punctuation: add small windows out of an enclosure.
  - Pinpointing: a landmark in the distance, a known there.
- Avoid straight, long, fully visible views, which Cullen calls "monotonous".
- Small misalignments matter. Offset openings a little rather than aligning them on one axis.

**Corrections to the earlier snippet summary**
- "Every there needs a here" has the dependency reversed. Cullen says that positing a HERE creates a THERE. The correct form is "you cannot have one without the other."
- Cullen says the straight road is monotonous because the "initial view is soon digested", not because it is straight as such.
- Serial vision needs uniform speed. It is a claim about how visibility changes under steady movement, not about cuts or pacing.
- Cullen treats serial vision as only one of three "gateways" (motion, position, content). The existing-and-emerging chain is normally accidental, and the designer's task is to take the linking over.
