# Field

A part of the [ontology](README.md). This page is the hub for the part: what it is, what the project currently holds about it, and the research filed under it. The studies and experiments stay the source; this page links to them.

## Definition

The environment entities are encountered in. An ordinary page maps vertical scroll to moving up and down a flat document. The field turns that plane by 90 degrees: scrolling up and down moves forward and backward in depth, and left and right rotates the view. The visitor moves through a site instead of reading down a document. In the field, an entity's thumbnail can already carry its media: sound, film, 3D.

Architectural reading: Landscape, walked as a *promenade architecturale*.

## Position

Deductions currently held, newest last. Each entry is dated and cites its evidence. Never rewrite an entry; add a new one that revises it.

- **2026-11-01:** Defined as above in the ontology conversation ([transcript](transcript.md)).
- **2026-11-05:** The field must be a place, not a void: a textured ground that recedes, entities that stand on it and occlude one another, a landmark visible from afar, and designed, slow pace. Placement must carry meaning; placement by id hash does not ([STU-0003](../../studies/0003-place-not-void/README.md), deductions 1, 2, 3, 8).
- **2026-11-05:** Things appear by being uncovered as the visitor moves at a steady pace, and moving back covers them again; a fade is not a reveal. Every entity touches the ground and the camera keeps one eye height, so the horizon works as a scale ([STU-0003](../../studies/0003-place-not-void/README.md), deductions 11, 12; after reading Cullen and Gibson in full).

## Open questions

- How does a visitor know where they are (wayfinding, "the void")? What anchors orientation: horizon, grid, fog, a trail?
- How is movement eased so it feels grounded and does not cause motion sickness?
- What can the *promenade architecturale* (thresholds, sightlines, sequence, pace) teach the field?
