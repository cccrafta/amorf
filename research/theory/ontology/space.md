# Space

A part of the [ontology](README.md). This page is the hub for the part: what it is, what the project currently holds about it, and the research filed under it. The studies and experiments stay the source; this page links to them.

## Definition

A field with a specific set of entities at specific coordinates: a collection of entities, arranged. A journey is also a space.

Architectural reading: Territory: knowledge as a site, not a database.

## Position

Deductions currently held, newest last. Each entry is dated and cites its evidence. Never rewrite an entry; add a new one that revises it.

- **2026-11-01:** Defined as above in the ontology conversation ([transcript](transcript.md)).
- **2026-11-04:** A space is made by the visitor: the entities set aside on the table, as arranged there, form a space. The field is, for now, one space of its own. To be tested in [EXP-0006](../../experiments/0006-thresholds-between-spaces/README.md).
- **2026-11-05:** A space needs a ground, an edge and a landmark, and is never shown whole on arrival. Entering it is a threshold: compression then release across light, sound, surface, direction and view over a short distance, not a fade ([STU-0003](../../studies/0003-place-not-void/README.md), deductions 1, 3, 4, 5; after round 1 of EXP-0006 failed).
- **2026-11-05:** A threshold reveals by occlusion at a steady pace, not by blending. Entities in a space stand on its ground and relate to each other across the route, not only to the visitor; the curator's arrangement on the table is what carries those relations in ([STU-0003](../../studies/0003-place-not-void/README.md), deductions 11, 12, 13; Cullen, Gibson, Castelvecchio read in full).
