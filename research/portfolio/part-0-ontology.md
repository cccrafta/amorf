# Part 0: What amorf is made of

The opening of the portfolio. Before any experiment, this part names the parts of amorf and the aim that holds them together. Later parts take one part each (field, coordinates, journeys, entities, and so on) and study it in depth. The source is the ontology draft: [../theory/ontology/README.md](../theory/ontology/README.md).

## Question

What is amorf, described in the terms of designers and architects rather than engineers?

## Position

amorf aims at a *temporal experience of discovery*: finding something as a process that unfolds over time, in the manner of a *promenade architecturale*, rather than an instant retrieval.

> amorf is a spatial archive for architecture and design, where the act of discovery is mapped as a journey through a navigable, multi-sensory landscape.

(Candidate tagline, not yet adopted.)

## The parts

| Part | What it is | Architectural reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entity | A node of knowledge with its own metadata and media of any type | Artifact |
| Field | Depth navigation: scroll moves forward and back, sideways rotates | Landscape, promenade |
| Coordinates | Where each entity sits in the field | Placement, proximity |
| Space | A field with specific entities at specific coordinates | Territory |
| Journey | The recorded sequence of a visitor's moves between entities; itself a space | Spatial sequence, footprint |
| Archive | Conventional, structured browsing of the same entities | Index, catalogue |

Full definitions: the hub page of each part, listed in [../theory/ontology/README.md](../theory/ontology/README.md#the-parts).

## Process

The ontology came out of one conversation, kept verbatim: [../theory/ontology/transcript.md](../theory/ontology/transcript.md). It moved from a dense one-line description, through a definition of "temporal experience", to a description of each part and a vocabulary that suits architects.
