# EXP-0006: Thresholds that make a space felt in the field

Follows [STU-0001](../../studies/0001-promenade-architecturale/README.md). First of five experiments drawn from it; the others (EXP-0007 to EXP-0010) assume a visitor can tell one space from another, which this one tests.

## Hypothesis

The ontology defines a space as a field with a specific set of entities at specific coordinates, read architecturally as territory ([space](../../theory/ontology/space.md)). Today the field is one continuous environment, so a space exists only as data. The field hub asks how a visitor knows where they are in "the void" ([field](../../theory/ontology/field.md)).

The promenade gives the answer architects use: a route is a sequence of distinct places joined by thresholds, and what you feel at the threshold is the change (STU-0001, notes on the houses and on Choisy). If that carries over, distinct atmospheres plus a marked crossing should be enough for visitors to feel territory.

It is wrong if visitors miss the crossing, or notice it but still describe one void.

## Method

1. Choose two or three groups of entities that belong together, one group per space.
2. Give each space one atmosphere that differs on a single axis at first (for example fog colour and density), so a result can be traced to one cause. Add ground, light or sound only in later rounds.
3. Build one kind of threshold between spaces. Candidates: a narrowing the camera passes through, a frame or gate, or a short band where the atmosphere blends. Start with one; try the others in later rounds.
4. Let each visitor walk the prototype without being told that spaces exist.
5. Afterwards ask: where did you go, how many places were there, and where did one end and the next begin? Record the answers and the journey track.
6. Note motion comfort at the threshold, since a narrowing changes how the camera moves.

Who the visitors are, and how many, is still to decide (see Next).
